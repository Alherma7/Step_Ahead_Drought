Hi @AJoel @meganomaly,

A clarification request on the relaunch rule, because I think it catches a construction that is probably very common on the current leaderboard, including in my own earlier submissions.

The rule says: "Any feature derived from, or encoding, data from month t+1 or later — regardless of source — is not allowed."

Test has 18 months. Six are fully observed (2015-09, 2016-01, 2016-06, 2016-12, 2018-07, 2018-11); the other twelve are ~99.8% masked. A natural and widely used baseline fills a masked row at month m with the mean of that grid cell's observed TWS_t across the test file.

For a row at 2016-02, that mean averages 2015-09, 2016-01, 2016-06, 2016-12, 2018-07 and 2018-11 — four of which are after 2016-02. So the fill encodes TWS information from months later than t+1, taken from the test features file itself rather than from an external source.

My reading is that this is caught by the rule as written, and that the only compliant use of the observed months is a strictly backward-looking anchor: for a row at month m, the last month <= m at which that cell is observed. I have rebuilt on that basis, and it costs real score — which is exactly why I would rather have it confirmed than assume.

Two questions:

1. Is the per-cell mean over all observed test months (i.e. including months after the row's own) disallowed under this rule?

2. If so, is the strictly-backward anchor (last observed month <= m for that cell, plus the forecast horizon) acceptable?

Also, minor and related: the update says latitude and longitude must not be used as features. Does that also rule out using (lat, lon) purely as a key — i.e. to look up that grid cell's own TWS_t history — as opposed to feeding lat/lon into the model as predictors? Every sensible use of TWS_t needs the cell identity.

Happy to share my reproduction either way.

Thanks,
