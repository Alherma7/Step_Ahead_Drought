Hi @AJoel @meganomaly,

The 13 August clarification resolved a lot. One line in it still decides whether a whole class of solutions is compliant, and it has now been asked three times — @ivarick on 22 July, @kbkb on 24 July, and again in "worries/questions" on 15 August — without an answer.

The clarification says:

    Permitted — "lat/lon as a lookup key — to retrieve a cell's own past TWS"
    Not permitted — "Raw coordinates, cell IDs, coordinate-derived encodings and spatial embeddings as predictive features."

Question: does the permission extend to other cells' strictly-earlier TWS?

Three concrete constructs, all reading TWS only at months ≤ t:

    Neighbourhood mean. The average anchor anomaly of every land cell within 500 km of the cell being predicted. The motivation is denoising, not location: GRACE's effective resolution is roughly 300 km while the grid is 1°, so adjacent cells carry nearly the same signal with largely independent noise. In our tests a 500 km average of the anchor is a better one-month predictor than the cell's own anchor (RMSE 0.637 → 0.604).
    Local deviation. The cell's own anchor minus that neighbourhood mean.
    A convolutional / grid-based model that takes the global TWS anomaly field at months ≤ t as an image and outputs the month t+1 field. It is never given lat/lon as a number, but it is spatial by construction.

Two readings, both defensible:

    A — these are "spatial embeddings" and are banned. Only a cell's own history may be used, and any convolutional or grid-based architecture is out.
    B — the ban targets using position as an identifier (letting a model memorise which cell is which). Averaging other cells' already-observed past values is ordinary spatial filtering of legitimately available data, and is permitted.

The two readings lead to completely different solutions, and rebuilding after the deadline is not possible. A one-line ruling would settle it:

    Neighbouring cells' TWS at months ≤ t — permitted, or not permitted?

And if the answer is not permitted: could you confirm that spatial filtering of external covariates (for example an ERA5 field averaged over a radius around the cell) remains permitted, given that the clarification explicitly allows sampling external gridded covariates by lat/lon?

Thank you.

Thanks for bringing this up.

Neighbouring cells’ TWS at months t or earlier is permitted. This includes spatial filtering or aggregation of past/current TWS values, such as neighbourhood means and local deviations. Spatial/grid-based models may also use the observed TWS field up to month t, provided they do not use observations from t+1 or later.

Coordinates may be used to identify cells, define neighbourhoods, or sample permitted gridded data, but raw coordinates, cell IDs, or coordinate-derived encodings should not be supplied to the model as predictive features.
