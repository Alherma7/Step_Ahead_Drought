Before building any model, I made three deliberately model-free calibration submissions constructed entirely from the currently distributed Train.csv/Test.csv (all-zeros; last-observed TWS_t carried forward; the same with a per-horizon linear shrinkage fitted on Train.csv). Their expected scores were pre-registered from training-file statistics before upload.

All three leaderboard scores are mutually consistent, but only under a single systematic correction: the covariance between the current files' TWS values and the hidden scoring targets is uniformly ~13% lower than the current files' internal structure implies, and the attenuation is the same at every forecast horizon (1 to 7 months). A genuine hydrological/period effect would generally vary with horizon; a uniform, horizon-independent attenuation is the signature of the targets being a different processing version of the same underlying field.

Also consistent with this, two accounts have now matched the benchmark (0.659470066) to nine digits after the relaunch, while (per the post above) a faithful re-implementation of the notebook recipe from the current files scores ≈0.90. That is only coherent if the hidden targets still follow the notebook-era ("v2") processing rather than the current files.

The key point is the shape of this pattern, not just the magnitude: all-zeros depends only on the target's marginal variance and shows a relatively small gap, while persistence depends on the covariance between TWS_t and Target and shows a much larger gap. That's consistent with shafi_miakhil's finding of a covariance-specific attenuation rather than a simple overall scale shift — a genuine distributional or seasonal effect would generally hit these two calibration methods more similarly. Back-solving from these two ratios, the implied covariance attenuation on my end comes out to roughly 20%, which is the same order of magnitude as the ~13% figure shafi_miakhil reported from their own pre-registered calibration submissions above, though not an exact match — could be a different download snapshot, or partly ordinary train/test drift rather than purely a version issue.

Adding an independent confirmation, from a different direction to the calibration probes above.

Using the all-zeros submission to pin the target's second moment on the public subset (RMSE 1.013783458 => E[y^2] = 1.027757), any submission's covariance with the hidden targets follows from its score: E[y*p] = (E[y^2] + E[p^2] - RMSE^2) / 2.

Applying this to my own submissions built entirely from the current files: my starter-notebook re-implementation scores 0.899886 (E[p^2] = 0.203, implied corr 0.461), and my best current-file model scores 0.743298 (E[p^2] = 0.464, implied corr 0.680).

Two things fall out. First, my re-implementation of the starter recipe on the current files scores 0.899886318 — matching @eugenius and @MICADEE to six decimals, from an independent implementation. That is three independent reproductions against a displayed benchmark of 0.659470066. Second, the gap is not a scaling problem. The optimal global rescale of my best model is c* = 1.012, worth +0.00005 — predictions from the current files are already optimally calibrated in scale and are simply less correlated with the hidden targets than the files' internal structure implies. A scale-only defect would be removable by a scalar, and it is not.

The benchmark at 0.659470066 implies corr ~0.76. Nothing built from the currently distributed files gets near that, while the starter recipe on the old files reportedly lands exactly on it.
